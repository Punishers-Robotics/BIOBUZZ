# BIOBUZZ 2026-2027: team judged award criteria

**Source:** Competition Manual TU10, Section 6.3, checked November 22, 2026.
See the [source and version register](../README.md).
All criteria below are paraphrased. IDs are local references.
Evidence suggestions are **editorial guidance**, not additional official criteria
and not claims that BIOBUZZ already has the evidence.

## Inspire

Source: Section 6.3.1, Table 6-2. Primary team target.

| ID | Official level | Criterion summary |
|---|---|---|
| INS-1 | Required | Submit a portfolio. |
| INS-2 | Required | Be a strong contender for at least one MCI award, at least one Team Attributes award, and Think. All three categories must be represented. |
| INS-3 | Required | Demonstrate a positive, inclusive team in which every member contributes to its success. |
| INS-4 | Required | Be able to share the team's experiences and knowledge with judges. |

The award description emphasizes a role-model team, FIRST Core Values,
Gracious Professionalism, sharing knowledge, and collective robot-development
success. These contextual themes do not replace the required criteria above.

Editorial guidance: assemble supported technical and team-attribute narratives,
show student contributions, and prepare students to explain their choices.
A polished PDF alone does not establish INS-2, INS-3, or INS-4.

## Think

Source: Section 6.3.2, Table 6-3. Primary team target.

| ID | Official level | Criterion summary |
|---|---|---|
| THINK-1 | Required | Submit a portfolio containing at least one of the four engineering topics below. |
| THINK-2 | Encouraged | Make the information easy to read and locate. |

The following are **alternatives within THINK-1**, not four separate mandatory
requirements. Covering more than one can strengthen the story when evidence exists,
but the official minimum is at least one.

| Local branch ID | Qualifying topic |
|---|---|
| THINK-1A | Evidence that the team used the engineering process. |
| THINK-1B | Robot-design lessons that were learned and applied. |
| THINK-1C | Alternatives compared, with reasons for the selected approach. |
| THINK-1D | Mathematics used to make robot or programming design decisions. |

Editorial guidance: show the engineering journey, not just the finished robot.
Connect a problem to alternatives, tests or analysis, a decision, and applied
learning. Use diagrams, measured results, and calculations only where supported.
Do not require invented numerical results for every story.

## Connect

Category: Team Attributes. Source: Section 6.3.3, Table 6-4.
A portfolio is not required for this award.

| ID | Official level | Criterion summary |
|---|---|---|
| CON-1 | Required | Explain a team-member skill-development plan with both learning goals and steps taken or planned to achieve them. |
| CON-2 | Encouraged | Build and maintain meaningful relationships with STEM professionals, in person or virtually. |
| CON-3 | Encouraged | Collaborate actively with the engineering community through mentoring, knowledge sharing, technical guidance, or joint work. |

Editorial guidance: link learning goals to actions and relationships, then show
what students gained or applied. A sponsor list or a one-time encounter alone
does not establish a meaningful, maintained STEM relationship.

## Reach

Category: Team Attributes. Source: Section 6.3.4, Table 6-5.
A portfolio is not required for this award.

| ID | Official level | Criterion summary |
|---|---|---|
| REA-1 | Required | Explain outreach objectives, the strategy behind activities, and how they support FIRST community growth. All three elements are required. |
| REA-2 | Required | Demonstrate successful recruitment of new teams, coaches, mentors, or volunteers who have not previously participated in FIRST. |
| REA-3 | Encouraged | Communicate effectively as a FIRST ambassador and increase public awareness of FIRST programs. |
| REA-4 | Encouraged | Create and continually improve outreach materials that introduce both the team and FIRST to the public. |

Editorial guidance: distinguish awareness, inquiries, and actual recruitment.
REA-2 lists teams, coaches, mentors, and volunteers; do not silently broaden its
wording to assume that new student membership alone satisfies it. Recruitment
records may support other claims too, but eligibility for this criterion needs
evidence of the stated outcome.

## Sustain

Category: Team Attributes. Source: Section 6.3.5, Table 6-6.
A portfolio is not required for this award.

| ID | Official level | Criterion summary |
|---|---|---|
| SUS-1 | Required | Explain plans for long-term success covering one or more of financial sustainability, season planning, or long-term team sustainability objectives. |
| SUS-2 | Required | Explain how progress toward sustainability plans and objectives is measured, reviewed, and tracked. |
| SUS-3 | Encouraged | Define team roles and an intentional process for preparing future student leaders. |
| SUS-4 | Encouraged | Identify organizational constraints, mitigate risks, and adapt plans when challenges arise. |

Editorial guidance: connect goals, responsibilities, progress reviews, and plan
changes. Fundraising totals alone do not show project management or long-term
sustainability. Retain only approved public financial summaries.

## Innovate, sponsored by RTX

Category: Machine, Creativity, and Innovation.
Source: Section 6.3.6, Table 6-7. A portfolio is not required for this award.

| ID | Official level | Criterion summary |
|---|---|---|
| INN-1 | Required | Show engineering work explaining how the design was developed. |
| INN-2 | Required | Present a creative or unique design of the robot or a mechanism. |
| INN-3 | Required | The creative design is stable and reliable and helps achieve game goals most of the time. |
| INN-4 | Encouraged | Explain how design risks were reduced. |

Editorial guidance: explain the creative solution and why it solves the challenge,
then show its development and supported reliability. Novelty language without
engineering history and performance evidence is insufficient.

## Control

Category: Machine, Creativity, and Innovation.
Source: Section 6.3.7, Table 6-8. A portfolio is required.

| ID | Official level | Criterion summary |
|---|---|---|
| CTL-1 | Required | Submit a portfolio identifying robot hardware or software control components, the challenges each component/system solves, and its function. All three elements are required. |
| CTL-2 | Required | Use at least one hardware or software solution with external feedback to control the robot and improve performance. |
| CTL-3 | Encouraged | Have control solutions that work consistently during most matches. |
| CTL-4 | Encouraged | Explain reliability through demonstration or an explanation of potential improvements. |
| CTL-5 | Encouraged | Describe engineering-process learning applied while developing sensors, hardware, algorithms, or combined control solutions. |

Editorial guidance: make the feedback loop understandable: input, decision,
action, challenge addressed, and observed behavior. Name the relevant sensors,
software, and mechanisms. Describe team-written work accurately and credit libraries.
An autonomous path or timed sequence alone does not establish external feedback.
The portfolio needs a system summary, not the entire codebase.

## Design

Category: Machine, Creativity, and Innovation.
Source: Section 6.3.8, Table 6-9. A portfolio is not required for this award.

| ID | Official level | Criterion summary |
|---|---|---|
| DES-1 | Required | Describe or demonstrate robot elegance, efficiency (simple construction and operation), and/or practicality of maintenance. |
| DES-2 | Required | Consider the whole robot or the detailed process used to create it, not only one component. |
| DES-3 | Encouraged | Stand out through appearance and function. |
| DES-4 | Encouraged | Explain the reasons behind design choices, including inspiration or function. |
| DES-5 | Encouraged | Have a consistent design aligned with the game plan or strategy. |

Editorial guidance: show whole-robot integration, access for maintenance, design
tradeoffs, and alignment with strategy. A mechanism detail can contribute to the
narrative but does not replace whole-robot coverage.

## Judges' Choice

Source: Section 6.3.9. Optional; not offered at every event.

This recognizes distinctive efforts, performance, or team dynamics that merit
recognition but do not fit the other award categories. Section 6.3.9 does not
provide a required/encouraged criterion table. Do not invent one or treat this as
an Inspire category substitute.

## Evidence assessment boundaries

Use these criteria to identify coverage and gaps, not to assign unsupported scores
or predict awards. An activity's folder does not establish award fit.
Keep each proposed connection traceable to the exact criterion and source record.
Read [judging and submission boundaries](portfolio-rules.md) before selecting
what must be visible in a portfolio versus explained or demonstrated in interviews.
