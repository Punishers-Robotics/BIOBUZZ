## Team principles: "put it in GEAR"

**Record status:** Draft; pending human review

The team uses "put it in GEAR" as a reminder to practice its principles in support
of FIRST's Gracious Professionalism and Coopetition. The expectations below are
summarized from the supplied principles, not an assessment of how consistently
they have been demonstrated.

### G: Growth mindset

- Be a "learn it all": stay curious, explore new ideas, and ask questions when
  something is unclear.
- Experiment freely and fail as quickly as possible; failed experiments are
  teachers. Treat mistakes as opportunities to learn, grow, and improve.
- Coaches and mentors should use **ESP: Explain, Show, Practice**, recognizing
  that students need instruction and practice before they can demonstrate a skill.

### E: Excellence

- Represent the team, town, state, and country with best efforts and Gracious
  Professionalism.
- Give your best in learning, practice, and competition; focus on the effort
  rather than trying to control the result.
- Work hard and do good work; what members gain depends on what they put in.
- Measure excellence against the team's own potential and the competition
  guidelines, not against other teams. The supplied principles express an
  aspiration to be among the best in the world.

The source also states, "have already proven we are! (MTI)." This is a reported
historical claim, not verified here. What MTI refers to, the event/date, and the
specific result supporting that statement have not yet been recorded.

### A: Accountability

- Own assigned responsibilities and see them through; the team depends on each
  member's contribution.
- Ask for help as far ahead of a deadline as possible if a task cannot be completed.
- Own mistakes that affect the team and apologize.
- Arrive at practices on time, RSVP for events, respond to communications, and
  communicate difficulties rather than suffering silently.

### R: Reach out

- Help teammates by sharing knowledge, offering encouragement, and being a good
  teammate.
- Help other teams through mentoring, sharing needed parts, and celebrating
  their successes.
- Help the community through outreach, fundraising, recruitment, and supporting
  event organizers.
- The team's stated Coopetition boundary is not to "cross pollinate" its
  strategies, status, or secrets to other teams, while helping those teams develop
  their own approaches and sharing learning and best practices. This records the
  team's wording and intent, not an official FIRST rule about information sharing.
